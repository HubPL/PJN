There are many ways to brew coffee, and each method gives a different taste. The most important factors are the grind size, the water temperature, the brewing time and the ratio of coffee to water. A common recommendation is about 60 grams of coffee per one liter of water, and water temperature between 90 and 96 degrees Celsius.

Espresso is made by forcing hot water through finely ground coffee under high pressure, usually about 9 bars. A single espresso takes around 25 to 30 seconds to prepare. Espresso was invented in Italy at the beginning of the 20th century, and it is the base for many drinks such as cappuccino, latte and americano.

A cappuccino consists of espresso, steamed milk and a thick layer of milk foam. A latte has more steamed milk and only a thin layer of foam. An americano is espresso diluted with hot water.

The French press uses coarsely ground coffee that steeps in hot water for about four minutes. Then a metal filter is pressed down to separate the grounds from the coffee. This method gives a strong coffee with a full body.

Pour-over methods, such as the V60 or Chemex, use a paper filter. Hot water is poured slowly over medium ground coffee. The paper filter removes oils, so the coffee tastes clean and light.

Turkish coffee is one of the oldest brewing methods. Very finely ground coffee is boiled with water and often sugar in a small pot called a cezve. The coffee is served unfiltered, so the grounds settle at the bottom of the cup.

Cold brew is made by steeping coarse coffee in cold water for 12 to 24 hours. It is less acidic and less bitter than hot coffee.
